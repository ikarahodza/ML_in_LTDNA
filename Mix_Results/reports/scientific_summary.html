Key Findings

1. TabPFN v2.5 achieves state-of-the-art performance without hyperparameter tuning

TabPFN v2.5 attained a mean balanced accuracy of 0.847 ± 0.045 across 10 seeds, representing a 15.3% relative improvement over the best result reported by Tan et al. (2025) for mixture EPGs (MLP: 0.735). All tree-based ensemble models (Extra Trees, LightGBM, Random Forest, XGBoost) also surpassed the Tan et al. ceiling, confirming that the performance bottleneck lies in classifier choice rather than the signal feature representation.

2. TabPFN is robust to class imbalance without explicit resampling

Applying ClassWeights or no resampling to TabPFN produced identical results (0.847 ± 0.045 for both), while SMOTE degraded performance to 0.753 ± 0.038. This indicates that TabPFN's pre-trained prior implicitly marginalises over class imbalance configurations, rendering external resampling redundant or harmful.

3. SHAP identifies biologically meaningful stutter-position features

UpStream_97/98 and DownStream_40/44 — corresponding to approximately two and one repeat units from the signal of interest — appear in the top features for all five independently trained model families. These positions align with the expected locations of back stutter (n−1) and forward stutter (n+1) products in tetranucleotide STR loci, providing model-agnostic evidence that classifiers have learned genuine biological relationships.

4. Forward Stutter remains the principal unresolved challenge

Forward Stutter is the smallest class (n=33 total; n=7 test instances per seed) and its feature-space neighbourhood is dominated by Allele (54% of top-5 prototypes) and Back Stutter (23%), with Forward Stutter itself appearing in only 17% of retrieved training examples. This distributional overlap is biologically expected and cannot be resolved without substantially more training data.

5. Multi-seed evaluation reveals the limits of single-seed benchmarking

Under 10-seed evaluation, traditional classifiers reproduced from Tan et al. achieved means of 0.548–0.712, below the corresponding single-seed values (0.546–0.735). The gap is attributable to hyperparameter tuning differences and the stochastic optimism of single-seed estimates. Crucially, our tree-based and TabPFN models exceed the Tan et al. best result even under this stricter protocol, making the advantage robust.
